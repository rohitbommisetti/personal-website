Most of the conversation about AI is about software companies. But most of the economy isn't software. It's services: consulting, healthcare administration, staffing, training, compliance, the long tail of firms whose product is the expertise of their people.

That's where I spend my time, and it's where I think the next decade of AI value gets created.

## Why services, why now

Service businesses have a few things in common:

- **Their margins are labor.** When a model can draft, review or research alongside a team, the economics change quickly.
- **Their data is unstructured.** Decades of reports, emails and call notes that were useless to traditional software are now useful.
- **Their customers buy outcomes.** Nobody cares whether a deliverable was written by a person or with a model. They care that it's right and on time.

## What actually works

The pattern I keep seeing isn't replacing people. It's giving a strong team leverage. The firms that win start with one painful workflow, measure it obsessively, and expand from there.

> The question isn't "how do we use AI?" It's "what would we do if this task took a tenth of the time?"

## What I'm watching

Pricing models that move from hours to outcomes, the rise of the AI-native services firm, and the companies brave enough to rebuild a workflow from scratch instead of bolting a chatbot onto the old one.
