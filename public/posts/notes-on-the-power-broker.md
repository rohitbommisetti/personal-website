Robert Caro's *The Power Broker* is about 1,300 pages on Robert Moses, the man who built much of modern New York without ever being elected to anything. It took me most of a summer. A few things stuck.

## 1. Power is built in the details

Moses understood the fine print better than anyone around him. He drafted legislation himself and buried the provisions that mattered where nobody else would read them. Mastery of boring details is an unfair advantage.

## 2. Momentum is a strategy

Moses would start construction before the funding was fully secured, knowing that a half-built bridge is hard to cancel. It's a ruthless tactic, but the underlying insight holds: once something visibly exists, the conversation changes.

## 3. Every project has people on the other side of it

The chapter on the Cross Bronx Expressway, "One Mile," is the heart of the book. Caro shows exactly who paid for the progress. Building things is a responsibility, not just an achievement.

## 4. Caro's own patience is the real lesson

He spent seven years on this book, and he's been writing about Lyndon Johnson for decades since. In a world optimized for speed, there is still enormous value in going deeper than anyone else is willing to.
